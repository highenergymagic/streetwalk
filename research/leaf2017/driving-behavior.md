# Driving behavior and traffic sound assumptions

The first-generation LEAF's [Nissan brochure](https://www.nissan-cdn.net/content/dam/Nissan/palestine/brochure/Nissan_Leaf.pdf) gives 11.5 seconds for 0–100 km/h. An [instrumented 2016 LEAF 30 kWh test](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15101006/2016-nissan-leaf-30kwh-instrumented-test-review/) measured 10.4 seconds to 60 mph. Those are performance runs. A simple numerical integration of Streetwalk's full-power EM57 force and drag curve gives about 11.5 seconds to 100 km/h and 10.8 seconds to 60 mph. Equipment, tires, state of charge and measurement method can shift actual times.

Routine virtual driving now requests about 1.5 m/s² at launch, tapering toward 0.8 m/s² at higher speeds. The acceleration command changes by at most 2 m/s³. Comfortable braking requests about 1.7 m/s², and route approaches use an earlier, more conservative 1.4 m/s² distance envelope. These values are deliberate driver-behavior assumptions, not Nissan specifications. [FHWA traffic-flow guidance](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/operations/tft/chap3.pdf) discusses roughly 0.6–0.7 m/s² comfortable acceleration above 48 km/h and about 1 m/s² unhurried deceleration at high speeds. [FHWA speed-management guidance](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/speed-management/guidelines-use-variable-speed-limit-systems-wet-weather/chapter-2-driver) cites 3.4 m/s² as a comfortable stopping-sight-distance deceleration threshold. Streetwalk stays below that during ordinary virtual stops but may snap to zero if a mapped stop is reached despite imperfect geometry or update timing.

TomTom Traffic Flow reports current speed, free-flow speed, confidence and closure for a road segment. It does **not** report live vehicle count. Streetwalk therefore estimates audible surrounding-car activity using mapped place density and road class as an urbanity proxy, plus a confidence-weighted nonlinear slowdown term when a TomTom segment matches the route. The sound engine turns that activity into approximately 5–26 audible pass-by events per minute, with randomized spacing. This is a perceptual soundscape control, not a count of passing real cars. Slow traffic may include queued cars that would not actually pass the virtual car; the estimate should be tuned by listening rather than interpreted as traffic volume.

The turn-indicator click is synthesized. Its roughly 0.68-second full cycle and left/right cabin bias are model choices, not a sampled 2017 LEAF relay. It starts within roughly five seconds of a route's left/right bearing change and stops after the turn. Route geometry can omit lane changes or give ambiguous roundabout bearings, so some maneuvers will not trigger it.
