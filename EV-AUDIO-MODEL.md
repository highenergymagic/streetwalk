# EV cabin audio model

Streetwalk renders headphone-oriented stereo audio from the virtual driver's seat. The drivetrain and motor curve approximate a 2017 Nissan LEAF; cabin transfer paths and road/wind sources remain synthetic rather than measurements of that exact car. Its sound should be judged by ear in the running app; the unit and rendered-signal tests verify structural properties, not perceptual realism.

## Evidence and design

- [EV run-up measurements](https://www.atna-mam.utcluj.ro/index.php/Acta/article/view/46) found road/tire and aerodynamic noise dominant, with discrete motor orders including 24 and 48. [Electric vehicle sound quality measurements](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/16/5567/xml) report motor masking as speed increases and greater high-frequency wind and tire content.
- [Nissan's first-generation LEAF brochure](https://www.nissan-cdn.net/content/dam/Nissan/palestine/brochure/Nissan_Leaf.pdf) specifies the EM57 motor at 80 kW and 254 Nm with an 8.1938:1 overall reduction and 205/55R16 tires. These set the rotor-speed and torque curves; loaded tire radius remains approximate. The older [ORNL teardown](https://info.ornl.gov/sites/publications/files/Pub46325.pdf) reports 7.93:1 for a 2012 car and is not used for the 2017 target. [UK government AVAS guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-noise-systems-to-stop-silent-electric-cars-and-improve-safety) describes the external warning sound below 20 km/h. The simulated interior motor is distinct from that external warning sound.
- [FHWA speed research](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/safety/speed-management/speed-concepts-informational-guide/chapter-5-speed-characteristics) describes distributions of chosen speeds rather than one universal speed. [FHWA response-time research](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/09049/index.cfm) shows individual response-time variation, and [naturalistic turn research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29203017/) reports varied right-turn speed profiles. These motivate variation in the simulated driver; the exact parameter ranges below are design assumptions rather than fitted coefficients from those studies.
- [FHWA pavement research](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/t504036.cfm) relates pavement texture to interior tire noise. [OpenStreetMap surface](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Surface) and [smoothness](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Smoothness) tags provide broad road categories, while mapped [traffic-calming features](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:traffic%20calming) mark some bumps and rumble strips. [Open-Meteo's Elevation API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/elevation-api) samples the Copernicus GLO-90 terrain model for approximate grade.
- [Wind buffeting research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003682X15000638) describes stationary broadband sound plus fluctuating gust and turbulence components. The renderer excites window-pressure resonances with stochastic airflow and two gust time scales. Closed-window transmission now uses two low-pass stages and attenuated airborne wind.
- The binaural filters use [MIT's measured KEMAR HRTFs](https://sound.media.mit.edu/resources/KEMAR.html), 128 samples at 44.1 kHz for 37 horizontal angles. Left/right symmetry covers the opposite side. Read `assets/hrtf/README.txt` for data provenance and attribution.
- [Automotive binaural response research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003682X23003158) describes the importance of near-field sound, uneven early reflections, and seat occlusion inside cars. Streetwalk uses measured free-field HRTFs for distinct dashboard, roof, rear and side-glass image-source paths. The path delays, absorption and strengths are approximate; this is **not** a measured in-car binaural response.
- [Granular EV interior sound research](https://snu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/design-and-evaluation-of-electric-vehicle-sound-using-granular-sy/) found that grain source, duration, envelope and RPM range affect listeners' judgments. Streetwalk now blends a transformed, [CC0 Nissan LEAF interior recording](https://freesound.org/people/martian/sounds/512492/) with the simulated source paths. Its model year and recording conditions are unknown, so it supplies irregular cabin texture only; provenance and processing are in `assets/ev/README.txt`.

## Signal path

1. **Drive motion and driver:** an approximate 1,520 kg 2017 LEAF with its 80 kW, 254 Nm EM57 motor, 8.1938:1 reduction, aerodynamic drag, and rolling resistance accelerates to the virtual road target. The 0.9 driveline efficiency and 3.0 m/s² full-power acceleration cap are model assumptions. Each drive samples a persistent driver style from a compact bell-shaped distribution. It correlates acceleration (roughly 1.05–1.85 m/s² at launch), braking (roughly 1.25–2.1 m/s²), jerk, cruise pace (87–100% of the mapped target), corner pace (roughly 82–119% of a base cap), and a roughly 1.7–2.5-second following headway. Small independent draws and per-turn variation prevent exact repetition. Small target-speed reductions cause a coast rather than immediate full braking. A sampled 0.5–1.25 second decision delay follows departure and mapped crossing waits. Kinematic braking reduces speed for turns, destination, crossing waits, and matched TomTom congestion. Route geometry is sampled ahead in 24 m steps; sustained heading change caps speed using lateral acceleration and the driver's braking envelope, so sweeping bends work even without a named maneuver. A synthetic lead car with a slowly varying speed and following gap adds stop–go motion during matched congestion. These ranges are plausible simulation choices, not calibrated driver population percentiles or actual vehicle tracks.
2. **Motor and regeneration:** wheel rotation from virtual speed and an approximate 0.316 m wheel radius drives the documented 8.1938:1 reduction. Vehicle acceleration, rolling resistance, drag, and route grade determine shaft torque. Grade also reduces available uphill acceleration. Open-Meteo's Copernicus GLO-90 terrain elevations are sampled every 100 m over roughly 8 km ahead and averaged over 150 m; the grade is capped at ±8% to limit DEM and route alignment errors. A lower eighth-order drive component supplies audible traction feedback during launch; 12th- and 24th-order components add detail. The interior whine receives an acceleration-dependent presence boost that tapers through 60 km/h, then fades linearly to zero over 1.5 seconds after acceleration or braking begins. A new acceleration or braking event starts a new fade. The centered path is tuned for a clearly audible launch burst; its drive component is 20% louder than the prior 0.8-second version. It enters equally through both cabin channels as a body vibration, without a fixed front-facing HRTF or directional reflection. Motor emphasis fades with speed as road and wind sound grow beyond about 20 km/h. A motor-mount low-pass and speed-dependent masking keep the tone under road sound at cruise. Regeneration grows during braking or descent. This is a single-speed RPM relationship, not a calibrated in-car sound curve from a 2017 LEAF.
3. **Tires and road:** the car moves over a fixed, distance-indexed multiscale road-height field. Front and rear wheels sample the same longitudinal profile 2.8 m apart; the two tire tracks share coarse bumps but differ in fine roughness. Changes in surface height excite body modes around 40, 80, and 150 Hz and a filtered airborne tire path. Longer 5-, 17-, and 53-metre roughness scales vary low rumble over time. Sparse position-fixed defects add occasional short jolts, including a low baseline probability on urban roads. Their occurrence probability increases nonlinearly as observed POI density within 1 km falls, adjusted by mapped road class. This is a proxy based on the downloaded neighborhood, not a measured road-condition feed or a 20-mile census. Most tire energy enters as a shared interior field; a smaller independent track component adds width without hard-panning each tire. Available OpenStreetMap `surface` and `smoothness` tags alter texture and bumpiness; mapped bumps, humps, cushions, tables and rumble strips cause one-shot body impacts, with a 2.8 m front/rear wheel delay for a hump. Untagged roads keep the earlier road-class and settlement proxy. Google mode uses neutral surface texture because it lacks road-class geometry.
4. **Wind:** apparent airspeed uses virtual speed and Open-Meteo wind speed and direction relative to heading. Dynamic pressure excites low-frequency window/body modes plus an attenuated airborne path. Two low-pass stages model closed glass and seals, removing the prominent high-frequency hiss. Two stochastic gust time scales vary pressure. Transmitted wind is shared across the cabin, with only a slight crosswind ear difference.
5. **Other cars and room tone:** synthetic tire/road pass-bys move past the listener. Each event lasts roughly 0.75–1.45 seconds, with a narrow peak as the car passes and two low-pass stages to mute high-frequency tire hiss through closed glass. Rain raises their filtered spray slightly. The pass interval corresponds to about 5–26 audible cars per minute, derived from a mapped urbanity proxy and a confidence-weighted, nonlinear TomTom current-speed/free-flow-speed ratio. TomTom Flow does not report a vehicle count, so this is an ambience estimate, not a traffic measurement; it has no individual vehicle positions. A softened dashboard-vent airflow and faint distant traffic continue at zero speed. The blower uses band-limited airflow with a subtle fan harmonic, and separate short binaural reflections from left and right dashboard vents. Its airflow level was reduced about 18%, while the fan harmonic and distant room layer remain. These sounds are **invented ambience**, not reports of actual cars.
6. **Recorded cabin texture:** an 80-second transformed LEAF interior excerpt supplies irregular tire, trim and cabin detail that the analytic sources miss. Two grains of independently randomized 8–12-second duration overlap at varied positions. Their summed envelope is normalized to reduce fixed-period level pulsing; a small rate variation prevents an obvious loop. Their gain follows virtual speed and road texture, and they fade at rest. The recording is already in-car, so it bypasses the outdoor HRTF.
7. **Turn indicators:** the next route maneuver selects a left or right signal when it is within about five seconds of travel, up to 140 metres. Engaging and releasing the stalk each produces a separate low mechanical clunk. The first relay tick follows after 0.12 seconds; subsequent ticks form a close pair separated by 0.23 seconds, then a 0.45-second gap. The clicks and clunks feed the cabin reflection field with approximately equal direct level at both ears, so they spread through the interior rather than pointing toward the selected side. The signal stops after the maneuver or when the drive is paused. The exact timing and timbre are synthesized choices, not a measured LEAF indicator recording.
8. **Spatial and cabin rendering:** passing cars pass through measured two-ear HRTFs, and their direction updates as they move. The direct motor whine enters both cabin channels equally, while other cabin components retain stereo variation. Tire and sealed-window noise are distributed through the interior mix and cabin reflections. The granular interior bed joins those sounds. Short delayed, material-filtered image paths arrive from the dashboard, roof, rear seating and both side windows. The vent blower also excites two separate dashboard paths; the roof and dashboard use measured KEMAR elevation responses. A quiet diffuse tail joins these paths without a long echo. The output is two distinct channels. Headphones convey the intended positions best; no head tracking is used.
9. **Rain and wipers:** when Open-Meteo reports rain, short windshield drops and two alternating filtered blade sweeps enter the front cabin field. Wiper rate rises with precipitation, while wet tires and passing-car spray grow modestly. These are synthesized events driven by a current weather estimate; neither actual wiper state nor rain on a particular block is observed.

All parameters change smoothly on the audio thread. A bounded final stage limits peaks. The cabin sound is paused when the app loses focus or the drive pauses, and stops when the drive ends. NVDA and the spatial POI cues use the normal audio mix alongside it.

## Limits

The HRTF is a generic dummy head and may not match an individual listener's ears. Body-mode frequencies and short reflections are approximate; source levels have not been calibrated to a particular vehicle, tires, road surface, weather sensor, cabin insulation, or playback system. The recorded texture comes from one vehicle and does not separately capture each road surface or speed; the live model still determines motion and changes. The road profile is synthetic, though fixed in space and sampled by actual wheel motion. TomTom flow only informs an approximate ambience density and a matched route speed; it does not identify nearby cars. The new lead car is a simulated response to flow, not an observed car. Open-Meteo wind and precipitation are grid estimates updated much more slowly than real gusts or showers. Copernicus terrain elevation can disagree with the road grade on bridges, tunnels and cuttings. A 25-second reference drive with a turn indicator and three seconds stopped can be generated at `target/ev-cabin-demo.wav` with `cargo test --release render_reference_drive -- --ignored` for headphone review. [Reference recordings and analysis](research/ev-recordings.md) document the listening targets and their limits.
